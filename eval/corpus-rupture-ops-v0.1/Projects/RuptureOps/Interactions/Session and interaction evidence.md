## Live exploration: Little Cloud

At 2026-07-11 21:01 UTC the player reported arriving at MRO "Little Cloud"
with a large amount of ammunition and consumables and a nearby claimed
Regeneration Chamber. The response combined map/spawn data with the player's
loss aversion and live position:

- overall difficulty: about 2/5;
- 13 persistent hostile spawn entries within 250 units;
- 8 tier-1 melee, 4 tier-1 ranged, and 1 tier-1 exploder;
- no mapped Goliaths, Flingers, tier-2 enemies, or raid-class concentration;
- markers describe possible persistent population, not simultaneous attackers;
- bank excess supplies first when returning to storage is reasonably quick;
- otherwise proceed methodically with the nearby respawn as useful but not a
  complete substitute for replacement equipment and a safe recovery cache.

The player completed Little Cloud and reported that it was "no big deal."
That direct observation belongs in play history without erasing the map-derived
threat description.

## New field evidence: first Geo Scanner

The player later reported a two-death failure chain followed by success:

1. A nearby claimed respawn point reduced travel distance but had no spare
   weapons or replacement materials, forcing a return to Landerworks.
2. The scanner fire base had turrets physically installed but not powered, so
   they did not defend the site.
3. Corruption then disabled the contested base and its claimed Regeneration
   Chamber, causing the next death to respawn at the starting launcher.
4. The successful attempt used a built-out fire base with every turret powered.
   The automated defense was a strong positive payoff.

The exact corruption threshold and universal Regeneration Chamber rule remain
unverified. The dictated food-loot report was ambiguous, possibly two good
food-related rewards or stacks and possibly a quantity of 20. No exact item or
count may be invented.

## Timer product and build interaction

At 2026-07-11 21:38 UTC the player requested a phone-first rupture timer that
would stay visible, count down to meaningful phases, warn visually and audibly
before ordinary enemies return, and show phase-specific gathering
opportunities. The resulting product research became the Watch Session design.

Three visual directions were produced: Field Console, Orbital Dial, and Mission
Timeline. When asked which one to implement, the player replied `3` at
2026-07-11 22:54 UTC. **Mission Timeline is the selected visual source of
truth.**

The implementation request was stronger than "scaffold an app": build the
feature, test it in an iPhone simulator, and stop only when it was ready for
phone installation. The task began on dirty `main`, kept new work under `ios/`,
and used parallel research lanes for deterministic cycle behavior and platform
concerns. At this fixture's cutoff there was no final handoff and no evidence
that the simulator/install-ready acceptance condition had passed.

## Interaction contracts exposed by this workload

- Preserve raw evidence before interpretation.
- Make bulk import replay idempotent across forked agents.
- Keep current, historical, planned, selected, unknown, and superseded state
  distinct.
- Pin numerical facts to both source snapshot and applicable game build.
- Combine semantic prose, structured rows, code, images, maps, and archives.
- Never mix coordinate frames without a verified transform.
- Support exact computation and spatial/relational queries without making
  derived output authoritative.
- Compress deep evidence into a terse field answer when the user is actively
  playing.
- Convert direct user feedback into durable learning while retaining
  uncertainty and counterevidence.
- Preserve a multi-goal queue when the conversation changes topic.
- Resume partial implementation from repository truth and explicit acceptance
  gates, without declaring unrun verification complete.
